\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Survivorship.}
The price feed lists firms as of October 2026. Firms that failed, were acquired or went dark are absent, which biases a long--short spread in a direction that is not fixed \citep{shumway1997}: a missing failure makes its leg's measured return too high and a missing acquisition makes it too low, so the effect on the spread depends on how each leg's missing names split between the two. We measured the \emph{final-year} channel for the monthly decile legs of the booster (2014--2023) on SEC filing dates and 8-K items, under fixed assumptions on the return at death (failure $-40\%$, acquisition $+20\%$, going dark $-20\%$): once blank-check companies are excluded, the central understatement of that spread is 0.2--0.45 percentage points per year, at most about 2.2 points across variants, and its sign is not identified (it flips if the short leg is acquisition-heavy within its bins); the weekly books of Section~\ref{sec:res_books} were not measured. The \emph{level} channel, that about two thirds of the firms filing in 2013 had stopped filing by 2026, is not bounded by this calculation. The models also load on the characteristics most flattered by survivorship (short-term reversal, idiosyncratic volatility); an ablation without them improves the ensemble out of sample, which is a diagnostic, not a candidate. A delisting-inclusive panel is the one change that would settle this.

\paragraph{Reused check window.}
The 2019--2022 window had been read three times before the graph experiments; the graph programme is its fourth use. Every statement about it is a statement about a reused window. The 2023 read is the only genuinely held-out year and is eleven months long; the frozen rule has 8--12\% power at the development-window IC of 0.024 (14--17\% at 0.03), so its pass is weak evidence as an event; the observed IC of $\numICTwentyThree$ is stronger evidence than the pass.

\paragraph{Price-level measurement error.}
The public feed's closes are split-adjusted at download date, and the panel builder undoes only the splits inside the loaded window, so the \$5 filter and the five price-level characteristics (\texttt{log\_price}, \texttt{log\_mcap}, \texttt{bm}, \texttt{ep}, \texttt{sp}) are wrong by the product of splits \emph{after} the window end for about 4--7\% of firm-months. This is a measurement error tied to future corporate actions, not a look-ahead on returns; a variant without the filter and the five characteristics replicates the out-of-window IC ($+0.050$ against $+0.057$).

\paragraph{Incomplete graph programme.}
The weekly C4 and C5 predictions, the C4 and C5 books, every paired $\Delta\IC$ against C4 and all of C5's paired $\Delta\IC$, and the weekly label-shift runs were not completed before the programme was paused, and the formal selection script was not executed. The reported graph conclusion rests on the completed cells: C0, C0g, C1, C2 and C3 with ICs, books and paired $\Delta\IC$ against C0 and C0g on all horizons; C4 and C5 with monthly and quarterly ICs only. For C1--C3 the missing comparisons cannot produce a pass, because none has a development $\Delta\IC$ against C0 with $t\ge2$ on any horizon; C5 remains open.

\paragraph{What the code does not fix.}
The booster's $\lambda$ and $\gamma$ and the pairwise objective's pair sampling are library defaults (xgboost 2.1.4); the deep models ran under torch 2.14.1. These are stated, not controlled.
