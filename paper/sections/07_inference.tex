\section{Inference}\label{sec:infer}

\paragraph{Rank information coefficient.}
Per date, $\IC_d$ is the Spearman correlation between prediction and $y^{ex}$ with average ranks on both sides; a date with a constant prediction contributes zero (a stable sort would otherwise rank the constant alphabetically and produce a spurious $\pm0.02$). Over $D$ dates,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ic}
\overline{\IC}=\tfrac1D\sum_d \IC_d,\qquad t=\frac{\overline{\IC}}{s_{\IC}}\sqrt D,\qquad \mathrm{ICIR}=\overline{\IC}/s_{\IC}.
\end{equation}
This $t$ treats dates as independent (the naive $t$); a cluster-robust version with refit years as clusters is reported where the number of clusters allows.

\paragraph{Paired $\Delta\IC$.}
For cells $A$, $B$ with seed-mean predictions, $\Delta_d=\IC_d(A)-\IC_d(B)$ on two bases: \emph{raw} (prediction against $y^{ex}$) and \emph{demeaned} (both demeaned within the SIC4 $\to$ SIC2 $\to$ division cascade of eq.~\eqref{eq:demean}, then Spearman). The demeaned basis asks whether the graph adds information \emph{within} industries, which is the only information the industry-neutral book can monetise. Formation values are averaged within calendar months and $t=\bar\Delta_{month}/(s_{month}/\sqrt{n_{months}})$.

\paragraph{Sharpe-ratio uncertainty.}
The iid standard error of an annualised Sharpe ratio from $n$ weekly observations \citep{lo2002} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:se}
SE(\widehat{\SR})=\sqrt{\frac{1+\widehat{\SR}_w^2/2}{n}}\sqrt{52},\qquad \widehat{\SR}_w=\widehat{\SR}/\sqrt{52};
\end{equation}
a block bootstrap with circular 13-week blocks and 2000 draws gives a dependence-robust alternative (cf.\ the stationary bootstrap of \citealt{politisromano1994}; \citealt{ledoitwolf2008}). Windows are pooled through the exact total-variance identity. For monthly books a block bootstrap of the \emph{centred} series (1000 draws) gives $p=(\#\{\bar x^*\ge\bar x\}+1)/(B+1)$ and a minimum detectable effect $\mathrm{MDE}=P\,(q_{0.95}(\bar x^*)-q_{0.20}(\bar x^*))$; a within-date permutation of the predictions through the same book code gives a second null, evaluated on the gross statistic because random books turn over more and would otherwise win on cost.

\paragraph{Selection across trials.}
The expected maximum of $N$ independent null Sharpe ratios with standard error $s$, $\E[\max_{k\le N}s\,Z_k]$ (20{,}000 draws), is the bar a selected best cell has to clear. The deflated Sharpe ratio \citep{baileylopez2014} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dsr}
\SR_0=\sqrt V\Big[(1-\gamma)\Phi^{-1}\big(1-\tfrac1N\big)+\gamma\,\Phi^{-1}\big(1-\tfrac1{Ne}\big)\Big],\qquad
\mathrm{DSR}_i=\Phi\!\Bigg(\frac{(\widehat{sr}_i-sr_0)\sqrt{n_i-1}}{\sqrt{1-\hat\kappa_3\widehat{sr}_i+\tfrac{\hat\kappa_4-1}4\widehat{sr}_i^2}}\Bigg),
\end{equation}
$\gamma$ the Euler-Mascheroni constant, $V$ the sample variance of the annualised net Sharpe ratios across the cells that ran, $N$ the declared count, per-period Sharpe ratios inside $\Phi$, and $\hat\kappa_3,\hat\kappa_4$ the cell's skewness and non-excess kurtosis. The superior-predictive-ability test \citep{white2000,hansen2005} is the correctly sized correction when every trial's return series is stored (in our calibration on synthetic series $\mathrm{DSR}(N)$ was conservative and SPA well sized); the 349-cell programme stored per-cell summaries, not series.

\paragraph{From information to Sharpe: cost ladder and IC multiple.}
For a weekly cell with gross annual return $\mu_g$, gross Sharpe ratio $S_g$, weekly turnover $\tau_w$ and declared cost $c_d$ per side, the stored net Sharpe ratios at 1, 2, 5 and 15 bps are reproduced to within 0.02 by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ladder}
\sigma=\frac{\mu_g}{S_g},\qquad \mathrm{drag}(c)=52\,\tau_w\,c,\qquad \SR_{net}(c)=\frac{\mu_g-\mathrm{drag}(c)}{\sigma},\qquad \hat c=\frac{\mu_g}{52\,\tau_w},
\end{equation}
$\hat c$ the break-even cost. Inverting gives the gross Sharpe ratio needed for a net target $S^\star$ at the declared cost and its ratio to the realised gross Sharpe ratio,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ic_mult}
S_g^{need}=S^\star+\frac{52\,\tau_w\,c_d}{\sigma},\qquad \kappa=\frac{S_g^{need}}{S_g},
\end{equation}
which we call the IC multiple. At fixed construction, volatility and turnover, gross return scales to first order with the IC \citep{grinold1989,grinoldkahn2000}, so $\kappa$ is a lower bound on the factor by which the information would have to grow. If $S_g<S^\star$ in every cell, it follows that no cost level reaches the target, since $\SR_{net}(c)\le S_g$ for $c\ge0$. The same quantities give an ex-post analogue of the transfer coefficient of \citet{clarke2002}, the ratio of the best realised gross Sharpe ratio to the zero-cost ICIR of the raw prediction. Factor exposures of realised books are checked by yearly OLS of the daily spread on $[1,\text{Mkt-RF},\text{SMB},\text{HML},\text{MOM}]$ with Newey-West errors \citep{famafrench1993,carhart1997,neweywest1987}.
