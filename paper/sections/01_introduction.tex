\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A model that ranks stocks by next-period return is judged by two numbers. The first is its information coefficient (IC), the cross-sectional correlation between its scores and realised returns, which is well documented and mostly encouraging \citep{gkx2020,chenpelgerzhu2024,kellymalamudzhou2024}. The second is the net Sharpe ratio of a book built from the scores, which is less well documented and less encouraging \citep{novymarxvelikov2016,avramov2023}. The fundamental law of active management links them \citep{grinold1989,clarke2002}: a Sharpe ratio is approximately the IC times the square root of breadth, the number of independent bets, times a transfer coefficient set by the portfolio construction, less a cost drag set by turnover. We measure each term of that identity on one dataset under a pre-registered protocol, which lets us answer a practical question: can a model built from free data reach a net Sharpe ratio of 1.5 with drawdown under 25\%, and do graph methods help?

Every input is free, with prices from a public survivor-only feed, fundamentals from SEC XBRL filings, earnings dates from 8-K filings, industry graphs from SIC codes and the BEA input-output tables, and customer-supplier links from filing text. A student can reproduce all of it, but the free price feed brings a survivorship bias that we bound in Section~\ref{sec:limits} and cannot remove.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A shallow booster with hyperparameters fixed in advance carries a rank signal whose sign replicates out of its development window and on one held-out year, whereas selecting hyperparameters per refit on a noisy validation slice removes its advantage over a linear control.
\item Along a 55-cell grid of constructions, an exact cost-ladder identity shows that the shortfall against the target comes from too little information rather than from cost. No gross Sharpe ratio reaches 1.5, so no cost level closes the gap, and to first order the least demanding cell would need at least about $\numKappaLeastBoth\times$ its IC.
\item On the development window, the three completed relational graph networks, trained end to end on point-in-time firm graphs, add a paired $\Delta\IC$ against an identical no-graph network that cannot be told from zero, and the rewired-graph comparison, which we completed only as monthly and quarterly IC levels, points the same way \citep{maslovsneppen2002}.
\item A power calculation puts confirmation of a Sharpe-0.3 property decades away, which leaves the sizing decision to a prior.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Related work.}
Trees and shallow networks are the strongest cross-sectional predictors on CRSP, with small out-of-sample $R^2$ and large pre-cost decile spreads \citep{gkx2020}, much of which costs and microcaps remove \citep{avramov2023}. Economic links predict firm returns \citep{cohenfrazzini2008,menzlyozbas2010}, and graph neural networks \citep{kipfwelling2017,hamilton2017,velickovic2018,schlichtkrull2018} are their end-to-end form. We know of few tests of them against a rewired-graph null on a point-in-time panel, and ours is partial (Section~\ref{sec:limits}). For inference under selection we follow \citet{harveyliuzhu2016}, \citet{baileylopez2014} and \citet{hansen2005}, for Sharpe-ratio uncertainty \citet{lo2002} and \citet{ledoitwolf2008}, and we read a small signal against the documented decay of published anomalies \citep{mcleanpontiff2016,houxuezhang2020}.
