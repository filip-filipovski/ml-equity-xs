\section{Power and the sizing decision}\label{sec:power}

How much forward data would it take to confirm a signal this small at its own level? We answer from the model's monthly IC series over 2009 to 2022 (168 months; mean $\numICMeanTwentyTwo$, standard deviation $\numICSdTwentyTwo$, lag-one autocorrelation $\numICLagTwentyTwo$) and from the Sharpe-ratio standard error of eq.~\eqref{eq:se}. For a one-sided 5\% test of $H_0$: mean $\IC\le0$, 80\% power needs $\numMonthsICThree$ months at a true IC of 0.03 and $\numMonthsICSix$ at 0.06. Telling the 2019 to 2023 level (0.06) from the long-run level needs at least as many, and six months of forward data have power $\numPowerSixMonthsICThree\pct$ and $\numPowerSixMonthsICSix\pct$ at the two levels. A block bootstrap of the same calculation agrees (Figure~\ref{fig:power}). For a book, confirming a Sharpe ratio of 0.3 against zero takes about $\numYearsSharpeThree$ years of monthly data, a Sharpe ratio of 1.0 about $\numYearsSharpeOne$ years.

The signal is present but cannot be confirmed at its own Sharpe level on any horizon that matters for an allocation decision. One strong year such as 2023 moves the posterior mean little against fourteen years at a Sharpe ratio of 0.1 to 0.2 (monthly decile book $\numDecileSharpe$, $\numDecileAnn\pct$ per year, $\MaxDD$ $\numDecileDD\pct$, 2009 to 2022). Sizing such a signal is therefore a prior decision rather than an estimate the data can deliver.
