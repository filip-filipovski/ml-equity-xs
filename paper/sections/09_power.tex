\section{Power and the sizing decision}\label{sec:power}

How much forward data would confirm a signal this small at its own level? From the model's monthly IC series over 2009--2022 (168 months; mean $\numICMeanTwentyTwo$, standard deviation $\numICSdTwentyTwo$, lag-one autocorrelation $\numICLagTwentyTwo$) and the Sharpe-ratio standard error of eq.~\eqref{eq:se}: for a one-sided 5\% test of $H_0$: mean $\IC\le0$, 80\% power at a true IC of 0.03 needs $\numMonthsICThree$ months and at 0.06 needs $\numMonthsICSix$; telling the 2019--2023 level (0.06) from the long-run level needs at least as many; six months of forward data have power $\numPowerSixMonthsICThree\pct$ and $\numPowerSixMonthsICSix\pct$ at the two levels. A block bootstrap of the same calculation agrees (Figure~\ref{fig:power}). For a book, confirming a Sharpe ratio of 0.3 against zero takes about $\numYearsSharpeThree$ years of monthly data, a Sharpe ratio of 1.0 about $\numYearsSharpeOne$ years.

The signal is not absent; it cannot be confirmed at its own Sharpe level on any horizon that matters for allocation. One strong year such as 2023 moves the posterior mean little against fourteen years at a Sharpe ratio of 0.1--0.2 (monthly decile book $\numDecileSharpe$, $\numDecileAnn\pct$ per year, $\MaxDD$ $\numDecileDD\pct$, 2009--2022). Sizing such a signal is a prior decision, not an estimate the data can deliver.
