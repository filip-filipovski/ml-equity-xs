\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We ask whether a cross-sectional return predictor fitted to free, point-in-time US equity data becomes a tradable long-short book, and whether message passing over firm graphs adds information to it. The protocol is pre-registered: a fixed shallow boosted-tree regressor on 28 characteristics, yearly expanding refits with an embargo, a development window 2009 to 2018, a check window 2019 to 2022, one hold-out read of 2023 and 349 pre-declared portfolio cells. For the graph question, relational GraphSAGE and graph-attention networks are trained end to end on SIC, customer-supplier, input-output and return-correlation graphs and compared by paired $\Delta\IC$ with an identical no-graph network and, as monthly and quarterly IC levels only, with the same network on degree-preserving rewired graphs. We find, first, that the rank signal is real and small and that it replicates out of window: weekly rank IC $\numICWeeklyDev$ on development, $\numICWeeklyChk$ on the check window, monthly $\numICTwentyThree$ on the 2023 hold-out. Second, no cell reaches a net Sharpe ratio of 1.5 on both windows, and since no cell's gross Sharpe ratio reaches 1.5 on either window, no cost level can close the gap. The selected cell nets $\numSelDevNet\pm\numSelDevSE$ on development ($\numSelDevAnn\pct$ a year, maximum drawdown $\numSelDevDD\pct$) and $\numSelChkNet\pm\numSelChkSE$ on the check window ($\numSelChkAnn\pct$, $\numSelChkDD\pct$), and the least demanding cell would need at least about $\numKappaLeastBoth\times$ its information. Third, on the development window the three completed graph networks add a paired $\Delta\IC$ that cannot be told from zero on any horizon. Because confirming a Sharpe-0.3 property at its own noise level would take decades, how much of such a signal to hold has to come from a prior rather than from the data.
\end{abstract}
