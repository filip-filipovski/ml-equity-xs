\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We ask whether a cross-sectional return predictor fitted to free, point-in-time US equity data becomes a tradable long--short book, and whether message passing over firm graphs adds information to it. The protocol is pre-registered: a fixed shallow boosted-tree regressor on 28 characteristics, yearly expanding refits with an embargo, a development window 2009--2018, a check window 2019--2022, one hold-out read of 2023 and 349 pre-declared portfolio cells. For the graph question, relational GraphSAGE and graph-attention networks are trained end to end on SIC, customer--supplier, input--output and return-correlation graphs and compared by paired $\Delta\IC$ with an identical no-graph network and, as monthly and quarterly IC levels only, with the same network on degree-preserving rewired graphs. Three results. (i)~The rank signal is real, small and replicates out of window: weekly rank IC $\numICWeeklyDev$ on development, $\numICWeeklyChk$ on the check window, monthly $\numICTwentyThree$ on the 2023 hold-out. (ii)~No cell reaches a net Sharpe ratio of 1.5 on both windows, and no cost level can close the gap because no cell's gross Sharpe ratio reaches 1.5; the selected cell nets $\numSelDevNet\pm\numSelDevSE$ and $\numSelChkNet\pm\numSelChkSE$, and the least demanding cell would need about $\numKappaLeastBoth\times$ its information. (iii)~On the development window the three completed graph networks add a paired $\Delta\IC$ indistinguishable from zero on every horizon. Confirming a Sharpe-0.3 property at its own noise level takes decades, so sizing such a signal is a prior, not an estimate.
\end{abstract}
