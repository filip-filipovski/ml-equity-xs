\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We ask whether a cross-sectional return predictor fitted to free, point-in-time US equity data becomes a tradable long--short book, and whether message passing over firm graphs adds information to it. The design is pre-registered: a fixed shallow gradient-boosted regressor on 28 characteristics fitted to cross-sectional return ranks, yearly expanding refits with an embargo, a development window 2009--2018, a check window 2019--2022 and one hold-out read of 2023; 349 pre-declared portfolio cells; and, for the graph question, relational GraphSAGE and graph-attention networks trained end to end on SIC, customer--supplier, input--output and return-correlation graphs, compared by paired $\Delta\IC$ with an identical no-graph network and, on the monthly and quarterly horizons, with the same network trained on degree-preserving rewired graphs (IC levels only; the paired comparison with the rewired null was not completed). Three findings. First, the ranking information is real and replicates out of its development window (weekly rank IC $\numICWeeklyDev$ on development and $\numICWeeklyChk$ on the check window; monthly $\numICTwentyThree$ on the eleven held-out months of 2023), but it is small. Second, no cell reaches a net Sharpe ratio of 1.5 on both windows, and no cost level can close the gap because no cell's gross Sharpe ratio reaches 1.5; the selected cell nets $\numSelDevNet\pm\numSelDevSE$ and $\numSelChkNet\pm\numSelChkSE$, and a fundamental-law decomposition shows that even the least demanding cell would need about $\numKappaLeastBoth\times$ its information ($\numKappaSelDev\times$ and $\numKappaSelChk\times$ for the selected cell). Third, on the development window every graph network's selected book is at or below the no-graph baseline, and paired $\Delta\IC$ against that baseline is indistinguishable from zero. A power analysis shows the property cannot be confirmed at its own Sharpe level on any practical horizon, so sizing such a signal is a prior decision, not an estimate.
\end{abstract}
