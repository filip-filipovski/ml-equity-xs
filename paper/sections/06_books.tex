\section{From predictions to a book}\label{sec:books}

\paragraph{Decile book.}
The simplest construction bins each date by the percentile rank of the prediction, holds the top decile long and the bottom decile short with equal weights within a leg (one dollar each side), and pays cost $c$ per side on the two-sided turnover $\tau_t=\|w^{new}-w^{prev}\|_1$ against the previous target weights:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decile}
g_t=\bar y_{long}-\bar y_{short},\qquad \mathrm{net}_t=g_t-c\,\tau_t .
\end{equation}
This is the book of Section~\ref{sec:power} and Table~\ref{tab:crisis}; the earlier window-grid study's decile book instead drifted the previous weights by the holding return, $w^{drift}_i=w_i(1+y_i)/(1+\bar y_{leg})$, before differencing.

\paragraph{The grid construction.}
The main grid uses a construction that controls the three exposures a rank book carries by default: volatility, industry and market beta. Let $m_t$ be the tradable set (present, finite score, universe filter, at least 50 names). The \emph{aim} $A_{i,t}$ is the centred rank of the prediction over all predicted names, $(R_i-\frac12)/n-\frac12$, or for the multi-horizon blends the mean of the W, M and Q (or W and M) centred ranks with the slower horizons carried forward from their latest formation. The aim is re-ranked over $m_t$ to $c_i$ and, with $\sigma_i$ the 63-session realised volatility (missing values to the median, floored at a fixed 5th-percentile constant), the weights are built by four maps:
\begin{align}
u_i&=c_i/\sigma_i,\qquad \mathcal N(v)_i=\frac{2\,(v_i-\bar v)}{\sum_{k:\,v_k\ne0}|v_k-\bar v|}\ \text{for }v_i\ne0,\quad \mathcal N(v)_i=0\text{ for }v_i=0,\label{eq:norm}\\
w^{(1)}&=\mathcal N(u),\qquad w^{(2)}_i=w^{(1)}_i-\overline{w^{(1)}}_{g(i)},\qquad w^{(3)}=\mathcal N(w^{(2)}),\label{eq:demean}\\
a&=\mathcal N\big(w^{(3)}-X(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top w^{(3)}\big),\qquad X=[\mathbf 1,\ \beta],\label{eq:neutral}
\end{align}
where $\bar v$ is the mean over the non-zero entries. The target $a$ has $\sum_i|a_i|=2$ (one dollar long, one short) and $\sum_i a_i=0$. The group $g(i)$ in eq.~\eqref{eq:demean} is defined on residual sets: the SIC4 group if it has at least three names; for names not demeaned at SIC4, the SIC2 group among those names if it has at least three; for the rest, the SIC division. Equation~\eqref{eq:neutral} is the orthogonal projection of the weights onto the complement of $\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf 1,\beta\}$, with $\beta$ the 252-session beta to the equal-weight universe, so the target's regression exposure to that beta is exactly zero ex ante: the residual already sums to zero over $m_t$, so the final $\mathcal N$ only rescales it, and $\sum_ia_i=0$, $\sum_i|a_i|=2$ and $a^\top\beta=0$ hold exactly. The industry demeaning of eq.~\eqref{eq:demean} holds only approximately after the projection, which subtracts a multiple of $\beta$ that is not constant within groups; and for $\theta<1$ the held book of eq.~\eqref{eq:partial} mixes targets built on earlier betas, so its beta exposure is small but not zero.

\paragraph{Partial adjustment, cost and statistics.}
With $\theta\in\{0.05,0.1,0.2,0.5,1\}$ and $\mathbb 1_{m_t}$ the tradable mask,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial}
w_t=\mathbb 1_{m_t}\odot\big(w_{t-1}+\theta(a_t-w_{t-1})\big),\qquad g_t=w_t^\top y^{raw}_t,\qquad \tau_t=\|w_t-w_{t-1}\|_1,\qquad \mathrm{net}_t=g_t-c\,\tau_t .
\end{equation}
Partial adjustment is a first-order filter on the aim: it trades a mean information lag of $(1-\theta)/\theta$ periods against turnover, and because $w_0=0$ and masking only removes weight, the held book always has $\sum_i|w_i|\le2$. Turnover is charged against undrifted previous weights; a drift-charged variant (weights drifted by realised returns before differencing) is also computed and, in every graph cell whose book was computed, changes the Sharpe ratio by at most 0.01. With $P=52$ weekly periods per year,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sr}
\widehat{\SR}=\frac{\bar x}{s_x}\sqrt{P},\qquad \mathrm{ann}=\bar x\,P,\qquad \MaxDD=\min_k\Big(\frac{C_k}{\max_{j\le k}C_j}-1\Big),\quad C_k=\prod_{s\le k}(1+x_s).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Grid, selection and pass rules.}
The grid has 55 cells: aims R1 (weekly), R2 (quarterly, carried forward and traded weekly), R4 (W+M+Q blend) and R4b (W+M blend) $\times$ universes ALL, LIQ, TOP500 (R2 has no TOP500) $\times$ five $\theta$. Declared cost per side is 15 bps for ALL, 5 bps for LIQ and 2 bps for TOP500, and every cell is also stored at 1, 2, 5 and 15 bps. \emph{Selection}: among cells with development $\MaxDD\ge-25\%$ at the declared cost, the highest development net Sharpe ratio. \emph{Pass}: the selected cell has net $\SR\ge1.5$ on development \emph{and} on the check window with $\MaxDD\ge-25\%$ on both. For a graph cell, a pass additionally requires, on every horizon the aim uses, a development paired $\Delta\IC>0$ (demeaned basis, Section~\ref{sec:infer}) with month-level $t\ge2$ against both the no-graph baseline C0 and the rewired null C4, and would be relayed only after an independent adversarial review with three pre-agreed reproductions (retraining without price-level features, drift-charged turnover, block-bootstrap standard errors). The selection and pass rules were written before the corresponding books were read: for the graph programme before any graph book, and for the 55-cell grid before its read on the rebuilt panels (the same grid had been read on development only on an older build).
